Flowers

Every flower here has carried its meaning for longer than any of us have been around. The quiz on the Discover page matches four of these to a mood and color.

Rose

The rose has carried the weight of human emotion for centuries love, loss, celebration, grief. Its layered petals suggest something guarded, capable of opening fully under the right conditions.

Sunflower

Sunflowers turn to follow the sun, which is probably why they've become shorthand for optimism. They don't do subtlety — just steady, open-faced warmth.

Lavender

Lavender has long been used to ease a racing mind in teas, in oils, in gardens planted near windows. Its softness isn't weakness; it's restraint.

Lotus

The lotus grows through mud to bloom clean on the surface, which is likely why it's tied to resilience across so many cultures. Less about avoiding difficulty, more about what comes after it.

Daisy

The daisy has always stood for simplicity a flower without much fuss, which is exactly its appeal. Its old meaning, "loyal love," fits something plain that keeps showing up anyway.

Tulip

Tulips bloom fast and briefly, which historically made them a symbol of a love that arrives suddenly. Their color, more than their shape, tends to be what people remember.

Moods

Six moods, and a short thought on each not a diagnosis, just a way of paying attention. The quiz on the Discover page matches four of these to a color and flower.

Happy

Happiness often arrives in small, unplanned moments sunlight through a window, a message from someone you missed, the first sip of good coffee. It doesn't usually ask to be earned. Noticing it, even briefly, tends to make it last a little longer.

Calm

Calm isn't the absence of feeling it's the space where feeling has room to breathe. It usually comes after slowing down: a walk with nowhere to be, a page read twice, silence that doesn't feel awkward.

Angry

Anger has a bad reputation, but it's often just information a sign that something matters more than you've let yourself admit. The useful part isn't suppressing it, but asking what it's actually pointing toward.

Tired

Tiredness is rarely just about sleep. Sometimes it's a quiet request to slow down, to say no to one more thing, to let a day be smaller than planned. Rest isn't a reward earned afterward it's part of the work itself.

Anxious

Anxiety tends to live in the future a meeting that hasn't happened yet, a reply that hasn't come. It's rarely solved by thinking harder about it. Naming it as "anxious" rather than just "bad" is usually the first useful step.

Grateful

Gratitude is one of the few moods that grows the more you look directly at it. It doesn't require anything going especially well just a willingness to notice what already is.
